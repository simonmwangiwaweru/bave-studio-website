# Bave Studio — Managing Your Website's Content

This is your guide to adding and updating everything on the site yourself — new
galleries, video links, testimonials, your bio, contact details. No developer
needed for any of this.

You can't break the site's design from here. This screen only lets you manage
content — text, images, links — never layout, colours or fonts.

## Logging in

Go to **https://bave-studio-cms.sanity.studio/** and sign in. (If you don't
have a login yet, ask for an invite — it takes two minutes to set up.)

You'll see a list of content types on the left: **Gallery / Project**,
**Testimonial**, **Streaming Case Study**, and **Site Settings**.

## Adding a photography or videography gallery

1. Click **Gallery / Project** → **Create new**.
2. **Title** — e.g. "Amina & David's Wedding" or "Regenesys Corporate Event".
3. **Category** — choose Photography, Videography, or Live Streaming. This
   decides which page it shows up on.
4. **Sub-category** (optional) — e.g. Weddings, Corporate, Portrait.
5. **Images** — upload photos here. For each one, fill in the **description**
   field (what's happening in the shot) — this isn't optional busywork, it's
   what lets the photo be found on Google and read aloud by screen readers for
   blind visitors.
6. **Video link** — for a videography project, paste the full YouTube or
   Vimeo URL here instead of (or alongside) images.
7. **Client name** / **Shoot date** / **About this project** — optional, but
   they add credibility if filled in.
8. **Show on homepage** — turn on for your strongest 2–3 projects only. This
   is what visitors see first, so don't turn it on for everything.
9. **Published** — must be on for it to appear on the site. Turning it off
   hides the project without deleting it, e.g. if a client asks you to take
   something down temporarily.
10. **Display order** — lower numbers show first within a category.
11. Click **Publish** (top right) — changes go live within a minute.

## Adding your showreel or a project video

Videos are never uploaded to the site directly — always paste a YouTube or
Vimeo link. This keeps the site fast, since large video files never touch it.

- **Showreel**: go to **Site Settings** → paste the link into **Showreel video
  link** → Publish. It appears at the top of the Videography page.
- **Individual project video**: add it as a Gallery with the video link field
  filled in (see above).

## Adding a testimonial

**Testimonial** → **Create new** → fill in the quote, the client's name, and
their organisation or the event name. Turn **Published** on, then Publish.
Testimonials you add here show up across the site automatically.

## Adding a live-streaming case study

**Streaming Case Study** → **Create new**. Fill in the event name, what was
required, what you delivered, a recording link if there is one, and viewership
numbers if you have them. This is the one section that's written as a story
rather than a gallery — live streams don't photograph well, so the case study
*is* the portfolio piece for this service.

## Site Settings — the one document that controls the rest of the site

**Site Settings** → there's only one of these. It holds:

- **Your photo (About page)** and a couple of **behind-the-scenes photos**
- **About page biography** — written in your own words
- **Phone**, **Email**, **WhatsApp number** (international format, e.g.
  `254798108543` — leave blank to hide the WhatsApp button)
- **Social media links**
- **Showreel video link**

Everything here is optional, but the more filled in, the more finished the
site looks. An empty field just doesn't show that section — nothing breaks.

## A few habits worth keeping

- **Curate, don't dump.** A tight set of 6–8 strong photos per gallery beats
  30 mixed-quality ones. This was the single biggest recommendation from the
  original site audit.
- **Always fill in image descriptions.** It's the one field that's easy to
  skip and actually matters for being found on Google.
- **Unpublish instead of delete** if you're ever unsure — you can always
  publish it again later, but a deleted image is gone for good.
